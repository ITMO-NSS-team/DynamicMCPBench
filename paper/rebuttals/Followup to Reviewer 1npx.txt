Follow-up to Reviewer 1npx

Two of the things we promised you are done, and one of them turned into a bigger result than the promise. Since the rebuttal stage is still open, here it is.

THE REFERENCE-VALIDATION GATE

You asked whether the distiller omits necessary effects. Our audit found one such spec in a hundred, we diagnosed it as an upstream reference-validation miss rather than a distillation one, and we said we would fix the task and tighten the validator. We did more than that: the gate is implemented, it runs at generation time, and the class of tasks it governs is now measured rather than asserted.

Over the full released corpus of 1,845 specs: **zero orphans**, 1,578 reference-consistent, 267 flagged. The flags fall into three buckets, 103 where the demanded value appears nowhere in the trace, 137 where it appears but out of the scope the checkpoint requires, and 27 with no final message to check against.

On the 750-task evaluation slice the gate governs **104 tasks, 13.9%**, and they behave exactly as a defect class should:

| | tasks | pass rate | never solved by any model |
|---|---|---|---|
| reference-consistent | 646 | 55.2% | 86 |
| flagged by the gate | 104 | 11.8% | 59 |

Flagged tasks are **4.3 times more likely to be unsolvable by every model**, and they account for **40.7% of the never-solved wall**, 59 of 145.

The part that matters for the paper is what happens when they are removed: **no model changes rank**. Every model gains roughly six points of pass^3 and the ordering is untouched. DeepSeek-V4-Pro goes 46.4 to 52.9, MiniMax-M3 42.4 to 48.1, Claude Haiku 4.5 41.1 to 47.1. So the defect class shifts the level uniformly and leaves the comparison intact, which is the property the paper actually relies on.

We would rather report this than quietly drop the tasks. It also sharpens the honest reading of the accuracies: they were lower bounds, and now we can say by roughly how much and why.

THE CROSS-MODEL CHECK, AND A RETRACTION

We told you we expected the scorer's conservatism to move the level rather than the ordering, and that we would validate it. We have, on three further models spanning 7 to 49 pass^3 against the annotated baseline, 240 judgments over six raters. It does not hold, and we would rather retract it here than let it stand.

| model | pass^3 | human passes a run the scorer failed |
|---|---|---|
| gemma4-31b | 42.5 | 66.7% |
| qwen3.6-35b (baseline) | 48.5 | 49.9% |
| qwen3-8b | 22.1 | 25.0% |
| smollm3-3b | 7.2 | 8.3% |

An eightfold spread, and reconstructing human pass rates swaps the top pair: 81.6% for gemma4-31b against 74.2% for qwen3.6-35b, where the scorer has them at 44.9 and 54.0.

What is going on is specific rather than general. Part is what a model fails on: capable models get further, so their failures land on value_produced, where the tool work was done but the final message missed the evidence predicate. Pooled, humans pass 64% of value_produced-only failures against 19% when a tool_effect is unmet. Standardising every model to a common failure mix still leaves 45 of the 58 points, so composition is about a quarter of it.

The rest looks like unlisted equivalent paths. Among tool_effect-unmet failures, smollm3-3b averages half a successful call on the runs a human passed: it is not judged harshly, it did nothing, and the human agrees. gemma4-31b averages three and still misses the checkpoint, the shape of a model reaching the effect by a route our equivalence set does not enumerate. That is the recall limit we flagged to you as unmeasured, now measured.

The scorer is not the variable: Tier-1 is deterministic code, identical for every model, and distiller families are evenly spread across the samples.

WHAT THIS CHANGES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

It changes the reading of the table. Accuracies are still lower bounds, as the paper says, but the bound is tighter for weaker models, so the published spread is compressed and two models a few points apart cannot be ordered by it. We will say exactly that.

It does not touch what the paper argues: that ground truth for an MCP-agent benchmark is a set of observable effects rather than an answer string or a tool list, and that this is what survives live, stateful data. This is a measurement of our instrument's resolution, and it gives both repairs an address: relax value_produced matching, and widen equivalence sets while measuring their recall. Neither can lower a score; both raise the floor unevenly, which is the finding.

Caveats we keep attached: 60 cards per model is about plus or minus 13 points, the pass stratum is 10 cards, and the baseline used the older three-question instrument, so it is not measured identically. The reordering is between two models 3.6 points apart, inside the range this result says should not be read as an ordering.
