Follow-up to Reviewer 1npx

Two of the things we promised you are done, and one of them turned into a bigger result than the promise. Since the rebuttal stage is still open, here it is.

THE REFERENCE-VALIDATION GATE

You asked whether the distiller omits necessary effects. Our audit found one such spec in a hundred, we diagnosed it as an upstream reference-validation miss rather than a distillation one, and we said we would fix the task and tighten the validator. We did more than that: the gate is implemented, it runs at generation time, and the class of tasks it governs is now measured rather than asserted.

Over the full released corpus of 1,845 specs: **zero orphans**, 1,578 reference-consistent, 267 flagged. The flags fall into three buckets, 103 where the demanded value appears nowhere in the trace, 137 where it appears but out of the scope the checkpoint requires, and 27 with no final message to check against.

On the 750-task evaluation slice the gate governs **104 tasks, 13.9%**, and they behave exactly as a defect class should:

| | tasks | pass rate | never solved by any model |
|---|---|---|---|
| reference-consistent | 646 | 55.2% | 86 |
| flagged by the gate | 104 | 11.8% | 59 |

Flagged tasks are **4.3 times more likely to be unsolvable by every model**, and they account for **40.7% of the never-solved wall**, 59 of 145.

The part that matters for the paper is what happens when they are removed: **no model changes rank**. Every model gains roughly six points of pass^3 and the ordering is untouched. DeepSeek-V4-Pro goes 46.4 to 52.9, MiniMax-M3 42.4 to 48.1, Claude Haiku 4.5 41.1 to 47.1. So the defect class shifts the level uniformly and leaves the comparison intact, which is the property the paper actually relies on.

We would rather report this than quietly drop the tasks. It also sharpens the honest reading of the accuracies: they were lower bounds, and now we can say by roughly how much and why.

WHAT IS NOW DONE

The per-category Tier-2 breakdown you asked for is finished. We replayed 4,500 Tier-1 tool_effect failures, 300 per category out of a population of 45,155, through four judge families with the prompt and schema held fixed, the same items to each.

The override rate is 4.93 percent for our default judge and 4.93 to 5.71 percent across the panel, a 0.78 point spread with overlapping intervals. Per category it runs from 1.83 percent on hard_neg to 9.83 percent on sibling, and that ordering is interpretable: sibling seeds interchangeable tools on the same server, hard_neg seeds adversarial near-duplicates. Nine failures in ten are unanimous NO across the panel; a majority overrides 3.85 percent.

Since Tier-2 only ever turns a failure into a pass, this also bounds how far any judge configuration could have moved the paper's numbers, and only upward. The published leaderboard remains deterministic throughout.
