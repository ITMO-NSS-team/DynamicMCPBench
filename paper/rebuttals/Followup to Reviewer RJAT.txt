Everything we promised you is now done, and your question about drift versus transient failure has a measurement behind it rather than a protocol.

YOUR QUESTION: DRIFT OR TRANSIENT FAILURE

We admitted the submitted study could not separate them, used a single retry, and that `broken` was an upper bound. We built the attributing classifier and ran the refresh wide: **113 servers and 12 domains** against 3 in the paper, a 38-fold widening.

The headline survives: **32.6% of recorded effects still reproduce identically** against 36.0% narrow, inside the band we registered in advance.

The composition of the remainder does not survive, which is the more useful result:

| outcome | share |
|---|---|
| identical | 32.6% |
| drifted, the call succeeds and the result changed | 67.0% |
| attributably broken | 0.4% |

The 0.4% is four calls, all schema drift, all on one server, zero state decay. A further 320 failed unattributably and are excluded rather than counted as breakage, the conservative direction.

So the 32% we reported as broken was an upper bound, loose by two orders of magnitude. Breakage is close to absent; drift is the whole phenomenon. The case for cached deterministic replay is stronger than the paper argued and rests on a different mechanism: the servers are not falling over, they are quietly returning different answers.

Per domain: `dev_tools` reproduces at 89.7%, `science_health` at 19.1%, a 70 point range on a 32.6% headline. Live re-scoring would be unfair and unfairly weighted by topic.

The preflight is implemented too: it derives credential, file, writable and table preconditions from the reference trace and quarantines a task whose environment cannot be provisioned **before any live call**, so it never reaches the agent-failure column.

Budget helps a great deal and never closes the gap: every model gains 12 to 19 points from k=4 to k=32 and stays 7 to 14 below curated. The binding constraint is reachability, not ranking: the retriever surfaces every required tool for 36.7, 48.0, 62.7 and 75.3 percent of tasks at k = 4, 8, 16, 32, and of the 1,064 unreachable single-attempt cells, **zero passed**. Three attempts at k=8 recover 16 to 22 percent of the deficit.

One correction to what we sent you. We wrote that single-step tasks were unaffected, on a 2.0 point difference from one model. Pooled over four models the gap is **12.8 points on short chains against 19.5 on long**. Depth costs about seven points more than shallowness; it does not account for the whole effect, and we should not have leaned on that single cell.

STILL OPEN

Nothing. The last item was Tier-2 override rates across judge families, broken down by category, and it is done.

We replayed 4,500 Tier-1 tool_effect failures, 300 per category from a population of 45,155, through four judge families. Prompt and schema are the paper's own, copied verbatim, so only the family varies, and the same items go to every family, so the comparison is paired.

| judge | override rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| claude-haiku-4.5 (our default) | 4.93% | 4.34 - 5.61 |
| gemma-4-26b | 5.42% | 4.80 - 6.12 |
| llama-3.3-70b | 5.33% | 4.71 - 6.03 |
| mistral-small-3.2-24b | 5.71% | 5.07 - 6.43 |

A 0.78 point spread, fully overlapping intervals, pairwise agreement 94.5 to 97.0 percent. Three of the four have open weights and authored no part of the corpus, so the panel is reproducible without a paid key.

Per category the rate runs from 1.83 percent on hard_neg to 9.83 percent on sibling, and that ordering is signal rather than noise: sibling seeds other tools on the same server, where an equivalent path usually exists; hard_neg seeds adversarially mined near-duplicates, where it usually does not. Whether an override is available is a property of the task's tool neighbourhood, and it varies an order of magnitude more than the judge family does.

Nine failures in ten are unanimous NO. A majority overrides 3.85 percent, all four 1.91 percent, and counting a single YES as enough would flip only 9.82 percent. Since Tier-2 only turns a failure into a pass, that bounds how far any judge configuration could have moved our numbers, and only upward.

One scope note: the prompt is held fixed. That is what makes the family comparison a comparison, and it leaves prompt sensitivity untested.
