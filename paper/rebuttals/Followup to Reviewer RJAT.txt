DRIFT VERSUS TRANSIENT FAILURE

We built the attributing classifier and ran the refresh over 113 servers and 12 domains against 3 in the paper, a 38-fold widening.

The headline survives: 32.6% reproduce identically against 36.0% narrow. The composition does not, which is the more useful result:

| outcome | share |
|---|---|
| identical | 32.6% |
| drifted, the call succeeds and the result changed | 67.0% |
| attributably broken | 0.4% |

Four calls, all schema drift, all on one server, zero state decay; 320 more failed unattributably and are excluded rather than counted as breakage. So the 32% we reported as broken was an upper bound loose by two orders of magnitude. Breakage is close to absent; drift is the whole phenomenon, and it makes the case for cached replay rest on a different mechanism than we claimed: the servers are not falling over, they are quietly returning different answers. Per domain, dev_tools 89.7% against science_health 19.1%, so live re-scoring would be unfair and unfairly weighted by topic.

The preflight is implemented: it derives credential, file, writable and table preconditions from the reference trace and quarantines a task whose environment cannot be provisioned before any live call, so it never reaches the agent-failure column.

TOOL DISCOVERY, EXTENDED

Four models, k in {4, 8, 16, 32}, a hierarchical router and the flat catalog, over 150 depth-balanced tasks with committed ids.

| exposure | haiku-4.5 | kimi-k2.6 | minimax-m3 | qwen3.7-max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| curated pool | 37.3 | 42.7 | 44.0 | 54.0 |
| rag k=4 | 16.7 | 21.3 | 18.0 | 22.7 |
| rag k=8 | 21.3 | 24.7 | 24.0 | 30.7 |
| rag k=16 | 24.0 | 33.3 | 31.3 | 38.0 |
| rag k=32 | 28.7 | 34.7 | 36.7 | 40.0 |
| hier | 28.0 | 32.7 | 32.7 | 36.0 |

Budget helps and never closes the gap: every model gains 12 to 19 points from k=4 to k=32 and stays 7 to 14 below curated. The binding constraint is reachability, not ranking. The retriever surfaces every required tool for 36.7, 48.0, 62.7 and 75.3 percent of tasks at k = 4, 8, 16, 32, and of the 1,064 unreachable cells zero passed. Three attempts at k=8 recover 16 to 22 percent of the deficit.

One correction. We wrote that single-step tasks were unaffected, on a 2.0 point difference from one model. Pooled over four, the gap is 12.8 points on short chains against 19.5 on long. Depth costs about seven points more than shallowness; it does not account for the whole effect, and we should not have leaned on that cell.

JUDGE FAMILIES, PER CATEGORY

4,500 Tier-1 tool_effect failures, 300 per category from a population of 45,155, through four families. Prompt and schema are the paper's own, copied verbatim, so only the family varies, and the same items go to every family.

| judge | override rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| claude-haiku-4.5 (our default) | 4.93% | 4.34 - 5.61 |
| gemma-4-26b | 5.42% | 4.80 - 6.12 |
| llama-3.3-70b | 5.33% | 4.71 - 6.03 |
| mistral-small-3.2-24b | 5.71% | 5.07 - 6.43 |

A 0.78 point spread, fully overlapping intervals, pairwise agreement 94.5 to 97.0 percent. Three of the four have open weights and authored no part of the corpus, so the panel is reproducible without a paid key.

Per category the rate runs 1.83 percent on hard_neg to 9.83 percent on sibling, an order of magnitude more variation than the family axis. That ordering is signal: sibling seeds other tools on the same server, where an equivalent path usually exists; hard_neg seeds adversarially mined near-duplicates, where it does not.

Nine failures in ten are unanimous NO; a majority overrides 3.85 percent. Since Tier-2 only turns a failure into a pass, that bounds how far any judge configuration could have moved our numbers, and only upward. One scope note: the prompt is held fixed, which is what makes this a family comparison and leaves prompt sensitivity untested.
