Follow-up to Reviewer sJ7917

You asked for the setting practitioners actually face: a large tool universe navigated with retrieval. We answered with one model at one retrieval depth. The full surface is now run, and since the rebuttal stage is still open we would rather you had it here than in a version you cannot see.

THE TOOL-EXPOSURE MATRIX

150 tasks from the released slice, balanced by reference-chain depth, task ids committed. Every condition exposes the full 1,168-tool catalog with no curated pool: four models, retrieval depth k in {4, 8, 16, 32}, a hierarchical router that picks a server before its tools, and the literal flat catalog. Baseline is the released curated verdicts for the same models on the same tasks, so no baseline compute was spent. Deterministic Tier-1 scoring throughout.

Retrieval budget helps a great deal and never closes the gap: every model gains 12 to 19 points from k=4 to k=32 and every model still sits 7 to 14 points below curated at k=32.

The binding constraint is reachability, not ranking. The retriever surfaces every required tool for 36.7, 48.0, 62.7 and 75.3 percent of tasks at k = 4, 8, 16, 32. Of the 1,064 single-attempt cells whose tasks are unreachable, **zero passed**. Three attempts at k=8 recover only 16 to 22 percent of the deficit, which is what one expects when the ceiling belongs to the tool surface rather than to the agent.

The ordering survives and the margins do not. Rank correlation with the curated leaderboard stays at or above 0.80 in every condition, but the spread collapses from 16.7 points under curation to 6.0 at k=4. Open exposure compresses the field rather than merely lowering it, so it distorts comparisons rather than deflating them uniformly.

The flat row is partial by design: the catalog serialises to roughly 292k tokens of tool schema, beyond the context window of three of these four models. Only 1M-context models can be offered it at all, which is itself worth reporting.

A CORRECTION TO WHAT WE SENT YOU

We wrote that the cost was entirely compositional and that single-step tasks were unaffected, resting on a 2.0 point difference from a single model. That does not survive the larger sample. Pooled over four models the gap is **12.8 points on short chains against 19.5 on long**. Depth costs about seven points more than shallowness; it does not account for the whole effect.

The single-model cell moved too, 30.7 against the 36.7 we reported, with curated at 54.0 against 57.3. Both draws follow the same recipe from the same 750-task slice; redrawing it 2,000 times puts them 0.94 sd apart and the paired deficit is 23.3 here against 20.6 before. The finding replicates. The earlier subset was an easier draw whose ids we had not committed; they are committed now.

WHAT THIS CHANGES

The conclusion is sharper than the one we sent you, and it is yours: **the exposure condition belongs to the benchmark, not to the agent.** It has to be specified, versioned and reported alongside results the way the task set is, and a pass rate that does not name its tool surface is not a complete claim. The revision says this and scopes the headline accordingly.

Separately, the equivalence-set share is now single-sourced and reproducible from the released corpus: 721 of 4,651 tool_effect checkpoints admit two or more tools, 15.5%, mean size 1.213, largest 12.
