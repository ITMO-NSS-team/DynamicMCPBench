THE TOOL UNIVERSE, MEASURED

You asked for the setting practitioners actually face: a large tool universe navigated with retrieval. We showed one model at one depth. Here is the surface around it.

150 tasks from the released slice, balanced by chain depth, ids committed. Every condition exposes the full 1,168-tool catalog with no curated pool. The baseline is the released curated verdicts for the same models and tasks, so no baseline compute was spent. Deterministic Tier-1 scoring, one attempt.

Retrieval budget helps a great deal and never closes the gap: 12 to 19 points gained from k=4 to k=32, still 7 to 14 below curated.

The binding constraint is reachability, not ranking. The retriever surfaces every required tool for 36.7, 48.0, 62.7 and 75.3 percent of tasks at k = 4, 8, 16, 32, and of the 1,064 unreachable single-attempt cells, zero passed. Three attempts at k=8 recover only 16 to 22 percent of the deficit, which is what one expects when the ceiling belongs to the tool surface rather than the agent.

The ordering survives and the margins do not. Rank correlation with the curated leaderboard stays at or above 0.80 in every condition, but the spread collapses from 16.7 points to 6.0 at k=4. Open exposure compresses the field rather than lowering it uniformly, so it distorts comparisons rather than merely deflating them.

The literal flat catalog runs only on the 1M-context models, since it serialises to roughly 292k tokens of schema; on minimax-m3 it scores 36.0, below its own k=32.

On the larger sample the cost is not flat across depth as our single-model cell suggested: the curated-to-open gap is 12.8 points on short chains against 19.5 on long. And the single-model cell itself reads 30.7 against 54.0 curated here, where we reported 36.7 against 57.3. Both draws follow the same recipe from the same slice; redrawing 2,000 times puts them 0.94 sd apart, and the paired deficit is 23.3 against 20.6. The finding replicates on a slice whose ids are now committed.

WHAT THIS CHANGES

The conclusion is sharper than the one we sent you, and it is yours: the exposure condition belongs to the benchmark, not to the agent. It has to be specified, versioned and reported alongside results the way the task set is, and a pass rate that does not name its tool surface is not a complete claim.
