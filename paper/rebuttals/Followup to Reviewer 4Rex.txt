DECAY ACROSS 113 SERVERS AND 12 DOMAINS

We rebuilt the refresh over 113 servers and 12 domains, against the 22 traces over three families in the paper. A 38-fold widening.

32.6% of effects still reproduce identically, against 36.0% on the narrow sample. A 3.4 point move, inside the band registered in advance. It is a property of the substrate, not of three hand-picked servers.

Per domain: dev_tools 89.7%, science_health 19.1%. A 70 point range on a 32.6% headline. Versioned reference content is nearly stable; listings that re-rank on every query are not.

THE REMAINDER IS DRIFT, NOT BREAKAGE

Re-run with a classifier that must attribute each failure rather than bucket it:

| outcome | share |
|---|---|
| identical | 32.6% |
| drifted, the call succeeds and the result changed | 67.0% |
| attributably broken | 0.4% |

Four calls, all schema drift, all on one server, zero state decay. 320 more failed unattributably and are excluded rather than counted as breakage. The 32% flagged as an upper bound resolves to 0.4%.

Drift is the whole phenomenon, which sharpens the argument for cached deterministic replay rather than merely supporting it: the servers are not falling over, they are quietly returning different answers, at a rate that depends on the topic. Live re-scoring would let a model's pass or fail turn on which domain a task touched and when it ran.
