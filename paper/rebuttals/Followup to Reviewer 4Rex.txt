Follow-up to Reviewer 4Rex

Both concerns are closed with data, and both of your instincts were right.

DECAY, WIDENED

You asked whether 36% holds across the 121 servers or varies by domain. We rebuilt the refresh over **113 servers and 12 domains** against the 22 traces over three families you flagged as thin, a 38-fold widening.

The headline holds: **32.6% of effects still reproduce identically** against 36.0% narrow, a 3.4 point move, inside the band we registered in advance. It is a property of the substrate, not of three hand-picked servers.

The domain spread runs as you guessed: `dev_tools` 89.7%, `science_health` 19.1%, a **70 point range** on a 32.6% headline. Versioned reference content is nearly stable; listings that re-rank on every query are not. The `dev_tools` figure rests on 29 resolved calls of 74, so read it as the most stable domain we measured, not a precise 90%.

WHAT THE WIDE RUN CORRECTED

We told you the submitted 32% `broken` was an upper bound. Re-run with a classifier that must attribute each failure rather than bucket it:

| outcome | share |
|---|---|
| identical | 32.6% |
| drifted, the call succeeds and the result changed | **67.0%** |
| attributably broken | **0.4%** |

The 0.4% is four calls, all schema drift, all on one server, zero state decay. A further 320 failed unattributably and are excluded rather than counted as breakage.

So the bound was loose by two orders of magnitude. Breakage is close to absent; drift is the whole phenomenon. That makes the case for cached deterministic replay stronger than the paper argued, on a different mechanism than we claimed: the servers are not falling over, they are quietly returning different answers, at a rate that depends on the topic. Live re-scoring would let a model's pass or fail turn on which domain a task happened to touch and when it ran.
