\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We argued that the right ground truth for an MCP-agent benchmark is neither
the final answer nor a list of tools, but the \emph{execution trace}, and we
built DynamicMCPBench around it: a reusable framework that runs over live
or user-supplied servers instead of shipping a fixed dataset, generates tasks
forward from real successful trajectories, and scores path-agnostic
\emph{effects} under deterministic replay, stratified by task category and
length. Because every required effect is
grounded in a successful call in the task's own reference trace---enforced at
generation and audited, not assumed---what a task demands stays tied to what a
working solution did, and because scoring reads effects rather than the reply,
the benchmark survives live, changing data. Running it over 121
servers and 24 models shows how far current agents have to go: even the
strongest solve only about half of the tasks, roughly a third are solved by
no model, and accuracy falls steadily as the tool chain lengthens; a human
study confirms the answer-agnostic scoring is reliable. We
release the framework and the generated benchmark so that practitioners can
rerun the entire pipeline on their own servers and models.
