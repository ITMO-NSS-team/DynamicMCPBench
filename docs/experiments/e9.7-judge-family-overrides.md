# E9.7 — Tier-2 override rates per category, across judge families

- status: done (2026-07-31)
- ledger: camera-ready commitment **3.2**
- promised to: reviewers **RJAT** and **1npx** — *"we will replay them through
  several judge families and report per-category rates in the camera-ready"*
- result: **positive** — the override rate is 4.9 to 5.7% across four judge
  families on identical items, a 0.78 pp spread with fully overlapping
  intervals, and it varies far more by task category (1.8 to 9.8%) than by which
  family is judging.

## Question / hypothesis

RJAT asked what varies when the underlying judge family changes, and how often
Tier-2 overrides a Tier-1 deterministic failure across the 15 categories. 1npx
asked for the same per-category breakdown. Our response conceded we had not
measured family variance and promised this run.

- **H1 (family invariance).** Override rates agree across families to within a
  few points on identical items. If they do not, the optional Tier-2 component
  is family-dependent and cannot be described as a neutral upgrade path.
- **H2 (category structure).** Rates differ by category in a way that tracks
  whether an equivalent path plausibly exists — highest where the corpus places
  interchangeable neighbours, lowest where distractors are adversarial.

Decision rule fixed before the run: **positive** if the between-family spread is
smaller than the between-category spread; **negative** if any family departs from
the others by more than the width of the category range.

## Method (+ reproduce command)

Every judged item is a **Tier-1 `tool_effect` failure** taken from the released
leaderboards, paired with the candidate's successful tool calls. The judge sees
one failed checkpoint and the candidate trace and answers one binary question.

**The prompt and the tool schema are copied verbatim from `dmcp/judge.py`**, so
the only thing that varies across the panel is the family. Temperature 0.

**Panel.** Three of the four authored no part of the corpus (whose goals,
explorations, distillations and validations came from openai, qwen, anthropic,
deepseek, z-ai, moonshotai and minimax), and those three have open weights, so
the panel is reproducible without a paid key. The paper's own default judge is
included as the reference point.

| judge | role | weights |
|---|---|---|
| `anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5` | the paper's default | closed |
| `google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` | authored no part of the corpus | open |
| `mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct` | authored no part of the corpus | open |
| `meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct` | authored no part of the corpus | open |

Two candidates were dropped after a smoke test: `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b`
(provider 502) and `cohere/command-r` (returns no tool call).

**Population and sample.** 45,155 Tier-1 `tool_effect` failures across 23 models
whose candidate traces are released. Sampling is capped at **300 per category**,
drawn round-robin across models so no model dominates a category, seed 0 —
4,500 items. **The same 4,500 items go to every family**, so the comparison is
paired rather than four separate estimates.

**Category.** Recovered from `provenance.goal_id` in the released specs, which
resolves 750/750 on the evaluation slice at exactly 50 per category. The earlier
belief that this was blocked because the saved judge records carry no category
field applied only to reusing those records, not to a fresh run.

```bash
uv run python scripts/e97_judge_panel.py --out evals/e97 --concurrency 24 --per-category 300
```

18,000 judgments; 1 call failed and is excluded.

## Result

**Positive.** The decision rule is met with a wide margin: the between-family
spread is **0.78 pp** against a between-category range of **8.0 pp**.

### By judge family, identical items

| judge | override rate | 95% CI | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| `claude-haiku-4.5` (default) | **4.93%** | 4.34 – 5.61 | 4,500 |
| `gemma-4-26b` | 5.42% | 4.80 – 6.12 | 4,499 |
| `llama-3.3-70b` | 5.33% | 4.71 – 6.03 | 4,500 |
| `mistral-small-3.2-24b` | 5.71% | 5.07 – 6.43 | 4,500 |

All four intervals overlap. Pairwise agreement on the shared items runs **94.5%
to 97.0%**, the lowest pair being the default judge against `mistral-small`.

### By category, pooled over the four families

| category | override rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| `sibling` | 9.83% | 8.3 – 11.6 |
| `decoy` | 8.83% | 7.4 – 10.6 |
| `stratified` | 7.75% | 6.4 – 9.4 |
| `homonym_trap` | 7.17% | 5.8 – 8.8 |
| `ambiguous_intent` | 5.67% | 4.5 – 7.1 |
| `prerequisite_strict` | 5.58% | 4.4 – 7.0 |
| `recovery_required` | 5.42% | 4.3 – 6.8 |
| `random` | 5.08% | 4.0 – 6.5 |
| `same_name` | 4.67% | 3.6 – 6.0 |
| `cross_server_alt` | 4.42% | 3.4 – 5.7 |
| `cross_domain` | 4.33% | 3.3 – 5.6 |
| `long_similar_chain` | 3.59% | 2.7 – 4.8 |
| `destructive_adjacent` | 3.58% | 2.7 – 4.8 |
| `complementary` | 2.50% | 1.8 – 3.5 |
| `hard_neg` | 1.83% | 1.2 – 2.8 |

H2 holds and the ordering is interpretable rather than noise. `sibling` seeds
other tools on the *same* server, so a genuinely equivalent path is most often
available; `hard_neg` seeds adversarially mined near-duplicates, where one
usually is not. The judge is discriminating, not guessing.

### Consensus across the panel

| families voting YES | items | share |
|---|---|---|
| 0 of 4 | 4,057 | 90.2% |
| 1 of 4 | 180 | 4.0% |
| 2 of 4 | 89 | 2.0% |
| 3 of 4 | 87 | 1.9% |
| 4 of 4 | 86 | 1.9% |

Nine in ten failures are unanimous NO. A majority overrides **3.85%** and all
four override **1.91%**. Even taking the most permissive reading, where a single
family's YES is enough, only **9.82%** of Tier-1 failures would flip.

## Conclusion & implication

Three things follow, and the first two are what the reviewers asked for.

**The choice of judge family does not move the result.** A 0.78 pp spread with
overlapping intervals and 94.5 to 97.0% pairwise agreement means the optional
Tier-2 component behaves the same whichever family runs it, including families
with open weights that had no hand in building the corpus.

**Per-category rates are now reported**, and they carry more signal than the
family axis by an order of magnitude. Whether an override is available is a
property of the task's tool neighbourhood, which is exactly what the category
taxonomy encodes.

**None of this changes any published number.** Tier-2 was never enabled for the
paper: the leaderboard, the rankings and the chain-length curve are deterministic
throughout. What this bounds is how much any judge configuration *could* have
moved them — under a majority rule, 3.85% of failed checkpoints, and every
override is a failure turning into a pass, so published accuracies remain the
conservative end.

Scope: one prompt. Prompt sensitivity is deliberately not tested here, because
holding the prompt fixed is what makes the family comparison a comparison. The
paper should say so rather than let "we varied the judge" imply both.
