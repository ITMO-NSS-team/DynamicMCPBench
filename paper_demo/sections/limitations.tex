\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several limitations qualify the system. First, \textbf{live-server flakiness}:
LIVE mode depends on third-party MCP servers that can be slow or unreachable,
which is why REPLAY is the default and every LIVE stage falls back to a frozen
fixture---so the most reliable path is not, strictly, live. Second,
\textbf{scorer conservatism}: the studio inherits the methodology's conservative
effect scorer, which accepts a run only when the required effects are
demonstrably present, so the verdicts shown are lower bounds on capability, not
a definitive judgment of an agent. Third, \textbf{a single-task default
showcase}: the booth centers on one worked example and three curated fixtures
chosen to make the verdict flip vivid, so it illustrates the disagreement rather
than measuring its prevalence (the parent study does the latter). Fourth,
\textbf{Tier-2 non-determinism}: the optional effect-equivalence judge is an LLM
and is kept off the demo's critical path; the headline verdict is Tier-1 only.
Finally, \textbf{Benchmark Advisor is a planning assistant, not an oracle}: its
MDE and power curves are conservative pre-run heuristics over declared
assumptions, not post-run proof, and cannot guarantee that public-log priors
transfer to a new MCP fleet or that missing outcomes are benign; final claims
still depend on the observed outcomes, the missingness policy, and the claim
boundary declared before generation.
